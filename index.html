How to build the journal once it's printed

  1. PrintCream or kraft paper, 100–120 gsm. Thinner shows through, thicker won't fold.
  2. Fold into signaturesTake 4–6 sheets, fold in half, nest them. That's one signature — 8 to 12 pages. Most journals use 3 to 5.
  3. Make a coverStiff card, an old book cover, or a box lid, wrapped in printed paper or fabric.
  4. StitchPamphlet stitch is the easiest: three holes along the fold, waxed thread, figure-of-eight. No needle? Bind on ribbon or rings.
  5. Fill itGlue in pockets, slide tags and cards into them, add envelopes and fussy cuts. This part takes the longest and matters most.
  6. DecorateLace, washi, buttons, and dangles — a hanging cluster of charms on thread down one side.

Common questions

What paper weight should I use?

100–120 gsm is the sweet spot. Standard 80 gsm copy paper shows the print through the other side; card over 160 gsm cracks at the fold. Cream or kraft stock gives you the aged look before you've done anything else.

Do I need a special printer?

No. Any home inkjet prints these at 300 dpi. Turn borderless printing on if your printer supports it, otherwise trim the white margin. A print shop works too if you'd rather not buy ink.

Can I sell journals made from these kits?

Most kits above include a commercial licence, which covers selling the finished physical journal. It does not cover reselling the digital files. Check the licence on each individual product page before you list anything.

What's the difference between a kit and ephemera?

A kit gives you full pages to fold into signatures — the body of the journal. Ephemera is the loose material that goes inside: tags, cards, envelopes, pockets, cut-outs. You need both, and most kits include some of each.

How long does one journal take?

An evening or two for a first attempt — printing, cutting and stitching are slower than they look. After that you'll do one in a couple of hours.
